Central Nervous System & Psychiatry

Horner's Syndrome >8 times
Classic clinical triad of ptosis, anhidrosis, and miosis, highly tested in relation to apical Pancoast lung tumors invading the sympathetic chain.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Ptosis (drooping eyelid)
  • Anhidrosis (lack of sweating)
  • Miosis (constricted pupil)

Diagnosis

  • Clinical observation of the classic triad
  • Cocaine drop test or Apraclonidine test
  • CT/MRI Chest to rule out Pancoast tumor

Treatment

  • Target underlying etiology
  • Surgical resection or chemo/radiation for Pancoast tumor
Trigeminal Neuralgia >9 times
Presents with severe unilateral lancinating facial pain in the distribution of CN V, treated first-line with Carbamazepine.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Severe unilateral, sudden, electric-shock-like facial pain
  • Triggered by light touch, chewing, or washing the face

Diagnosis

  • Clinical evaluation based on pain presentation
  • MRI Brain to rule out secondary causes (e.g., Multiple Sclerosis, vascular compression)

Treatment

  • First-line: Carbamazepine
  • Second-line: Oxcarbazepine, Baclofen
  • Definitive/Refractory: Microvascular decompression
Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS) >10 times
Acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy showing ascending paralysis, often following Campylobacter jejuni bloody diarrhea; respiratory support and monitoring is the most critical survival step.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Symmetric, ascending muscle weakness/paralysis
  • Areflexia and sensory paresthesias
  • Autonomic instability and potential respiratory failure

Diagnosis

  • Lumbar Puncture (CSF analysis): Albuminocytologic dissociation (high protein, normal WBCs)
  • Nerve Conduction Studies (NCS) showing demyelination

Treatment

  • Respiratory monitoring (FVC, NIF) as priority
  • First-line: IV Immunoglobulin (IVIG) or Plasma Exchange (Plasmapheresis)
  • Note: Corticosteroids are ineffective
Myasthenia Gravis >10 times
Autoimmune neuromuscular junction defect featuring fluctuating ptosis and diplopia that worsens at night; diagnosed with AChR antibody test and managed first-line with Pyridostigmine.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Fluctuating fatigable muscle weakness
  • Ptosis and diplopia worsening toward the end of the day
  • Bulbar symptoms (dysphagia, dysarthria)

Diagnosis

  • Anti-AChR antibody test (most specific)
  • Edrophonium (Tensilon) test or Ice pack test
  • CT Chest to screen for Thymoma

Treatment

  • First-line symptomatic: Pyridostigmine
  • Chronic/Severe: Prednisone or immunosuppressants
  • Crisis: IVIG or Plasmapheresis
  • Surgical: Thymectomy
Cerebral Myelopathy >8 times
Spinal cord compression (such as cervical stenosis) presenting with upper motor neuron signs in lower limbs (hyperreflexia, Babinski) and lower motor neuron signs in upper limbs.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • UMN signs in lower limbs (spasticity, hyperreflexia, Babinski sign)
  • LMN signs in upper limbs (atrophy, fasciculations, hyporeflexia)
  • Gait instability and sensory deficits

Diagnosis

  • MRI Spine (Gold standard) to show cord compression and signal change

Treatment

  • Surgical decompression (e.g., laminectomy or cervical discectomy)
  • Physical therapy and supportive care
Brown-Séquard Syndrome >9 times
Spinal cord hemisection presenting with ipsilateral loss of proprioception/vibration and UMN signs below the lesion, alongside contralateral loss of pain and temperature.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Ipsilateral motor paralysis and UMN signs below level of lesion
  • Ipsilateral loss of vibration and tactile/proprioceptive sensation
  • Contralateral loss of pain and temperature sensation starting 1-2 segments below lesion

Diagnosis

  • Clinical neurological mapping
  • MRI Spine to localize hemisection/etiology

Treatment

  • High-dose IV steroids in acute traumatic spinal cord injury (controversial)
  • Surgical stabilization / decompression
Werdnig-Hoffmann Disease >7 times
Autosomal recessive degeneration of anterior horn cells presenting as a floppy baby with hypotonia and symmetric weakness at birth.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Floppy infant syndrome with severe symmetrical weakness
  • Tongue fasciculations and absent deep tendon reflexes
  • Sparing of sensory systems

Diagnosis

  • Genetic testing for SMA1/SMN1 gene deletion
  • EMG showing denervation

Treatment

  • Disease-modifying therapies: Nusinersen, Risdiplam, or Onasemnogene abeparvovec
  • Supportive respiratory and nutritional management
Syringomyelia >8 times
Cystic cavity (syrinx) within the spinal cord damaging the anterior white commissure, classically presenting with a bilateral "cape-like" loss of pain and temperature sensation over the shoulders.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Bilateral cape-like sensory loss (pain and temperature) across hands, arms, and shoulders
  • Preserved fine touch, vibration, and proprioception
  • Associated with Chiari I malformation

Diagnosis

  • MRI Cervical Spine (Gold standard showing syrinx)

Treatment

  • Surgical decompression (e.g., posterior fossa decompression)
  • Placement of a syringoperitoneal shunt if progressive
Tabes Dorsalis >7 times
Late manifestation of tertiary neurosyphilis presenting with progressive degeneration of the posterior columns, leading to sensory ataxia and Argyll Robertson pupils.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Sensory ataxia with positive Romberg sign
  • Lancinating/lightning pains
  • Argyll Robertson pupil (accommodates but does not react to light)
  • Loss of vibration and proprioception

Diagnosis

  • CSF VDRL test (highly specific)
  • Serum FTA-ABS or TPHA

Treatment

  • First-line: IV Aqueous Penicillin G for 10–14 days
Epidural Hematoma >10 times
Biconvex/lenticular intracranial hemorrhage secondary to middle meningeal artery rupture; managed via urgent surgical evacuation.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Head trauma (pterion fracture) with lucid interval followed by rapid deterioration
  • Ipsilateral dilated pupil (CN III compression) and contralateral hemiparesis

Diagnosis

  • Non-contrast CT Head showing hyperdense biconvex/lenticular lesion not crossing suture lines

Treatment

  • Urgent surgical craniotomy and hematoma evacuation
  • ICP management (e.g., Mannitol, hypertonic saline)
Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH) >12 times
Classically presents as the "worst headache of my life" (thunderclap) secondary to a ruptured berry aneurysm, diagnosed initially on non-contrast CT head.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Sudden-onset severe "thunderclap" headache
  • Meningismus (nuchal rigidity) and altered mental status

Diagnosis

  • Initial: Non-contrast CT Head
  • Next step if CT is negative: Lumbar Puncture showing xanthochromia

Treatment

  • Endovascular coiling or surgical clipping of aneurysm
  • Nimodipine to prevent vasospasm
Brain Abscess >9 times
Ring-enhancing lesion presenting with focal neurological deficits post-dental extraction or procedure, caused by oral anaerobes, Strep viridans, or S. aureus.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Fever, focal neurological deficits, and headache
  • Increased ICP (vomiting, papilledema)

Diagnosis

  • Contrast CT / MRI Brain showing single or multiple ring-enhancing lesions
  • Note: Lumbar puncture is contraindicated due to risk of herniation

Treatment

  • Empiric IV antibiotics: Ceftriaxone + Metronidazole (+/- Vancomycin)
  • Surgical aspiration or drainage for lesions >2.5 cm
Bacterial Meningitis >11 times
High fever, neck stiffness, and altered sensorium; CSF analysis demonstrates elevated neutrophils, high proteins, low glucose, and elevated opening pressure.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Triad: Fever, nuchal rigidity, altered sensorium
  • Positive Kernig and Brudzinski signs

Diagnosis

  • Lumbar Puncture (CSF Analysis): Neutrophilic pleocytosis, high protein, low glucose (<40% of serum)

Treatment

  • Empiric: Ceftriaxone + Vancomycin (+ Ampicillin for infants/elderly)
  • Adjuvant: Dexamethasone prior to or with first dose of antibiotics
Abducens (CN VI) Nerve Palsy >8 times
Often secondary to cavernous sinus thrombosis or increased ICP, resulting in horizontal diplopia and medial deviation of the affected eye.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Inability to abduct the affected eye
  • Horizontal diplopia worsening on lateral gaze toward affected side
  • Medial strabismus at rest

Diagnosis

  • Clinical cranial nerve exam
  • MRI Brain and Orbits to check for tumor, stroke, or cavernous sinus pathology

Treatment

  • Treat underlying cause (ICP reduction, control diabetes, infection management)

Alzheimer's Disease >8 times
Primary degenerative dementia of the elderly, with early-onset cases strongly linked to Down Syndrome (Trisomy 21).
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Progressive memory loss (short-term first), apraxia, agnosia
  • Cognitive decline interfering with daily activities

Diagnosis

  • Clinical diagnosis via mini-mental status exam (MMSE)
  • MRI Brain showing diffuse cortical atrophy, especially hippocampi

Treatment

  • Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors: Donepezil, Rivastigmine, Galantamine
  • NMDA receptor antagonist: Memantine
Broca's Aphasia >7 times
Expressive, non-fluent motor aphasia where speech is limited to a few words, caused by damage to Broca's area in the frontal lobe.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Non-fluent, slow, halting speech with intact comprehension
  • Frustration due to awareness of deficit
  • Associated right hemiparesis (arm/face)

Diagnosis

  • Bedside language evaluation
  • CT / MRI Brain showing dominant inferior frontal gyrus lesion (MCA territory)

Treatment

  • Speech and language rehabilitation therapy
  • Treatment of underlying ischemic/hemorrhagic stroke
Saturday Night Palsy >8 times
Radial nerve compression against the humerus shaft, presenting with wrist drop and sensory loss over the dorsal first web space.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Wrist drop (inability to extend wrist and digits)
  • Loss of sensation over the 1st dorsal interosseous web space

Diagnosis

  • Clinical examination
  • EMG / Nerve Conduction Velocity (if non-resolving)

Treatment

  • Conservative: Cock-up wrist splint

Ulnar Nerve Injury >9 times
Compression of the ulnar nerve as it passes through Guyon's canal, resulting in an ulnar claw hand deformity.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Ulnar claw deformity (hyperextension of MCP and flexion of PIP/DIP in 4th/5th digits)
  • Sensory loss over medial 1.5 digits
  • Positive Froment's sign

Diagnosis

  • Clinical assessment and Tinel's sign over Guyon's canal / cubital tunnel
  • Electromyography (EMG)

Treatment

  • Splinting and activity modification
  • Surgical decompression / transposition if severe or refractory

Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Weakness in arm abduction beyond 15 degrees (deltoid motor loss)

Diagnosis

  • Clinical exam following shoulder dislocation/fracture

Saturday Night Compression (Neurapraxia) >8 times
Prolonged radial nerve compression leading to neurapraxia, evaluated clinically with Tinel's sign.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Motor deficit without nerve transection
  • Temporary loss of motor function (wrist drop)

Diagnosis

  • Positive Tinel's sign over compression site
  • Nerve Conduction Velocity (NCV) demonstrating conduction block

Treatment

  • Supportive management and physical therapy

Anorexia Nervosa >10 times
Eating disorder characterized by food restriction, intense fear of gaining weight, body image distortion, and a BMI < 18.5 kg/m².
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Severe emaciation (BMI < 18.5)
  • Amenorrhea, lanugo hair, bradycardia, cold intolerance

Diagnosis

  • Clinical DSM-5 criteria

Treatment

Binge eating followed by purging, causing a metabolic alkalosis with hypokalemia.

Clinical Features

  • Recurrent binge eating with compensatory behaviors (vomiting, laxatives)

Diagnosis

  • DSM-5 criteria

Static encephalopathy presenting in early childhood with delayed milestones, scissoring gait, and sustained clonus, linked to birth asphyxia or prematurity.

Clinical Features

  • Motor developmental delay, spastic hypertonia

Transverse Myelitis >8 times
Immune-mediated destruction of the spinal cord showing a combination of bilateral motor, sensory, and autonomic (bladder/bowel) dysfunction, showing T2 hyperintensity on MRI.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Rapidly progressive paraparesis/quadriparesis
  • Defined sensory level sensory loss

Diagnosis

  • MRI Spine with Contrast showing T2 hyperintensity and cord enhancement

Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA) Toxicity >9 times
Presents with anticholinergic symptoms, prolonged QRS interval, convulsions, and coma; treated first-line with Sodium Bicarbonate.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • 3 Cs: Coma, Convulsions, Cardiac arrhythmias
  • Anticholinergic signs (mydriasis, dry skin, urinary retention)

Diagnosis

  • 12-lead ECG showing QRS duration >100 ms and right axis deviation

  • Supportive airway management and IV fluids

Cardiovascular & Respiratory Systems

Aortic Stenosis >12 times
Crescendo-decrescendo late systolic ejection murmur radiating to the carotids, associated with a weak and delayed distal pulse (pulsus parvus et tardus).
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Classic Triad (SAD): Syncope, Angina, Dyspnea on exertion
  • Crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur at right upper sternal border radiating to carotids
  • Pulsus parvus et tardus

Diagnosis

  • Echocardiogram (Gold standard) assessing aortic valve area and gradient

Treatment

  • Definitive: Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement (SAVR) or Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR)
Aortic Dissection >11 times
Excruciating, sudden-onset tearing chest pain radiating to the back in a hypertensive male; gold-standard imaging is CT angiography showing a widened mediastinum.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Sudden severe, tearing/ripping chest pain radiating to interscapular back
  • Blood pressure asymmetry between arms (>20 mmHg)

Diagnosis

  • Initial: Chest X-ray (widened mediastinum)
  • Diagnostic Gold Standard: CT Angiography Chest/Abdomen or TEE

Treatment

  • Medical: IV Beta-blockers (Esmolol, Labetalol) to reduce HR and BP
  • Stanford Type A: Emergency surgery
  • Stanford Type B: Medical management usually
Dressler Syndrome >8 times
Autoimmune fibrinous pericarditis presenting 2 to 10 weeks post-myocardial infarction with pleuritic chest pain and fever.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Pleuritic chest pain relieved by sitting forward
  • Fever, malaise, pericardial friction rub weeks post-MI

Diagnosis

  • ECG showing diffuse ST elevation and PR depression
  • Echocardiogram showing pericardial effusion

Treatment

  • First-line: High-dose Aspirin or NSAIDs + Colchicine
  • Avoid other NSAIDs immediately post-MI if possible; steroids for refractory cases
Prinzmetal (Vasospastic) Angina >8 times
Transient ST-segment elevation during chest pain, triggered by coronary vasospasm and treated first-line with Calcium Channel Blockers.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Episodic chest pain at rest, often occurring at night or early morning
  • Unrelated to exertion

Diagnosis

  • ECG during episode showing transient ST elevations
  • Coronary angiography ruling out obstructive CAD

Treatment

  • First-line: Calcium Channel Blockers (e.g., Diltiazem, Amlodipine) and Nitrates
  • Contraindicated: Non-selective Beta-blockers
Second-Degree AV Block Mobitz Type II >10 times
ECG demonstrates dropped QRS complexes without prior PR interval prolongation; managed definitively with a pacemaker.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Lightheadedness, syncope, fatigue, or dyspnea
  • Risk of progression to complete heart block

Diagnosis

  • 12-lead ECG: Constant PR intervals followed by unexpected non-conducted P waves

Treatment

  • Temporary: Transcutaneous or transvenous pacing
  • Definitive: Permanent pacemaker implantation
Dilated Cardiomyopathy >10 times
Systolic heart failure showing a ballooned heart, caused by chronic alcohol abuse, wet beriberi (thiamine deficiency), or cocaine use.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Signs of systolic heart failure (dyspnea, orthopnea, peripheral edema)
  • S3 gallop and cardiomegaly

Diagnosis

  • Echocardiogram demonstrating enlarged chambers and low ejection fraction (<40%)

Treatment

  • Standard HF regimen: ACEi/ARBs/ARNI, Beta-blockers, Spironolactone, SGLT2i
  • Abstinence from alcohol/toxins
Septic Shock >11 times
Drowsy state with warm peripheries, persistent hypotension, and lactic acidosis despite adequate fluid resuscitation; requires aggressive IV fluids and vasopressors.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Hypotension (MAP <65 mmHg) refractory to fluid resuscitation
  • Tachycardia, warm extremities early on, altered mental status

Diagnosis

  • Elevated Serum Lactate (>2 mmol/L)
  • Blood cultures and source identification

Treatment

  • 30 mL/kg IV crystalloids
  • First-line vasopressor: Norepinephrine
  • Empiric broad-spectrum IV antibiotics within 1 hour
Coarctation of the Aorta >8 times
Congenital narrowing of the aorta, differentiated by upper extremity hypertension and weak femoral pulses with radio-femoral delay.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Upper extremity hypertension with lower extremity hypotension
  • Weak, delayed femoral pulses (radio-femoral delay)
  • Rib notching on X-ray

Diagnosis

  • Echocardiogram or CT/MR Angiography

Treatment

  • Infants: Prostaglandin E1 (Alprostadil) to keep ductus arteriosus open
  • Definitive: Surgical repair or balloon angioplasty with stenting
Left Anterior Descending (LAD) Occlusion >14 times
The most common site of coronary occlusion, presenting with anteroseptal ST elevation in leads V1-V2 or V1-V4.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Severe crushing retrosternal chest pain radiating to jaw/arm
  • High risk of cardiogenic shock and acute heart failure

Diagnosis

  • 12-lead ECG showing ST elevation in leads V1–V4
  • Elevated Cardiac Troponins

Treatment

  • Primary Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) within 90 minutes
  • Dual antiplatelet therapy (Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor), anticoagulation
Legionnaires' Disease >9 times
Severe atypical pneumonia caused by Legionella pneumophila (classically associated with cruise ships or water systems), presenting with relative bradycardia, confusion, and dry cough.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • High fever with relative bradycardia (Faget sign)
  • Hyponatremia, diarrhea, confusion

Diagnosis

  • Urinary Legionella Antigen Test
  • Chest X-ray showing patchy or lobar infiltrates

Treatment

  • First-line: Azithromycin or Levofloxacin
Crepitations (Crackles) >9 times
Abnormal lung sounds indicating alveolar fluid or inflammation, serving as a hallmark sign of interstitial lung disease or pneumonia.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Fine or coarse crackles on auscultation
  • Dyspnea, hypoxia, cough

Diagnosis

  • Auscultation

Treatment

  • Diuretics (Furosemide) if pulmonary edema

Lobar Pneumonia >10 times
Presents with high fever, rusty sputum, dull percussion, and bronchial breathing on auscultation.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Productive cough with rusty sputum (S. pneumoniae)
  • Increased tactile fremitus, dullness to percussion, bronchial breath sounds

Diagnosis

  • Chest X-ray demonstrating lobar consolidation
  • Sputum culture and Gram stain

Treatment

  • Outpatient: Amoxicillin, Azithromycin, or Doxycycline
  • Inpatient: Ceftriaxone + Azithromycin

Characterized by a PaO2 < 60 mmHg with normal or low PaCO2, commonly seen in ARDS or pneumonia.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Cyanosis, tachypnea, tachycardia, dyspnea

Pancoast Tumor >9 times
Apical lung cancer eroding the first rib, presenting with shoulder pain radiating down the arm (ulnar distribution) and Horner's syndrome.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Severe shoulder and arm pain (C8-T1 nerve root involvement)
  • Ipsilateral Horner's syndrome

Pulmonary Embolism >11 times
Sudden dyspnea and pleuritic chest pain, classically in a postpartum, pregnant, or bedridden patient; diagnosed via CTPA.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Sudden-onset dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, hemoptysis
  • Tachypnea, tachycardia, signs of DVT

Diagnosis

  • Initial screening: D-dimer (if low probability)
  • Diagnostic Gold Standard: CT Pulmonary Angiography (CTPA)

Treatment

  • Anticoagulation: LMWH, UFH, or DOACs (Rivaroxaban, Apixaban)

Gastroenterology, Hepatology & General Surgery

Emphysematous Cholecystitis >10 times
Life-threatening form of acute cholecystitis caused by gas-forming anaerobes (e.g., Clostridium perfringens), requiring immediate emergency cholecystectomy.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Severe RUQ pain, fever, systemic toxicity, commonly in elderly diabetic males

Diagnosis

  • Abdominal Ultrasound / CT showing gas in the gallbladder wall or lumen

Treatment

  • Emergency laparoscopic or open cholecystectomy

Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Clinical Features

  • Painless obstructive jaundice, weight loss, palpable gallbladder (Courvoisier sign)

Diagnosis

  • CT Pancreas (Pancreatic protocol)
  • Tumor marker: CA 19-9

Treatment

  • Surgical: Pancreaticoduodenectomy (Whipple procedure)
Toxic Megacolon >10 times
Severe complication of Ulcerative Colitis presenting with bloody diarrhea, systemic toxicity, and colonic dilation on abdominal X-ray; treated via subtotal colectomy.

Clinical Features

  • Clinical intraoperative assessment
