Cardiovascular & Respiratory Systems

Myocardial Infarction >15 times
Focuses on the Left Anterior Descending (LAD) artery occlusion, ECG changes, complications like ventricular rupture, and markers like Troponin or CK-MB.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Ischemic necrosis of myocardium secondary to acute coronary artery thrombosis following atherosclerotic plaque rupture.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Crushing substernal chest pain radiating to left arm/jaw, diaphoresis, dyspnea, S4 gallop, or cardiogenic shock.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: 12-lead ECG (ST elevation in LAD/anterior leads V1-V4).
Diagnostic: Cardiac Troponin I/T (most specific) and CK-MB.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: MONA protocol (Morphine, O2, Nitrates, Aspirin) + P2Y12 inhibitor.
Definitive: Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) within 90 minutes.

Acute Pericarditis >10 times
Focuses on sharp chest pain relieved by sitting/bending forward, widespread concave ST-segment elevation, and PR depression.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Acute inflammation of the pericardial sac, most commonly idiopathic or post-viral (Coxsackievirus B).

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Sharp pleuritic chest pain relieved by leaning forward; pericardial friction rub on auscultation.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: ECG showing widespread concave ST-segment elevation and PR-segment depression.
Diagnostic: Echocardiogram to assess effusion.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: High-dose NSAIDs (Ibuprofen/Aspirin) + Colchicine.
Definitive: Treatment of underlying cause; pericardiocentesis if tamponade occurs.

Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy (HOCM) >8 times
The most common cause of sudden death in young athletes.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Autosomal dominant disorder of sarcomere proteins causing asymmetric septal hypertrophy and LVOT obstruction.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Exertional dyspnea, syncope, sudden cardiac death; crescendo-decrescendo systolic murmur increasing with Valsalva.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: ECG (LVH, deep Q waves).
Diagnostic: Transthoracic Echocardiogram showing asymmetric septal hypertrophy (>15 mm) and SAM of mitral valve.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Beta-blockers (Metoprolol) or Non-dihydropyridine CCBs (Verapamil).
Definitive: Surgical septal myectomy or ICD placement for high-risk patients.

Rheumatic Fever >12 times
Focuses on molecular mimicry against M protein, migratory arthritis with subcutaneous nodules, and long-term valvular sequelae.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Type II hypersensitivity autoimmune reaction following Group A Strep pharyngitis mediated by M protein antibodies.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Jones Criteria: Joint arthritis (migratory), Carditis, Subcutaneous Nodules, Erythema Marginatum, Sydenham Chorea.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Rapid Strep test / Throat culture.
Diagnostic: Elevated ASO titer or Anti-DNase B plus 2 Major or 1 Major + 2 Minor Jones criteria.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Penicillin G / Amoxicillin + NSAIDs for arthritis.
Definitive: Prophylactic Benzathine Penicillin G every 3-4 weeks to prevent recurrent valvular heart disease.

Atrial Fibrillation >10 times
Recognised by an irregularly irregular pulse with absent P waves, and its high risk of systemic thromboembolism.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Supraventricular tachyarrhythmia characterized by disorganized atrial electrical activity and chaotic ventricular response.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Palpitations, fatigue, lightheadedness, irregularly irregular peripheral pulse, pulse deficit.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial & Diagnostic: 12-lead ECG demonstrating absence of P waves and irregularly irregular QRS complexes.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Rate control (Beta-blockers/Diltiazem) + Anticoagulation (DOACs based on CHA2DS2-VASc).
Definitive: Cardioversion (electrical/pharmacological) or catheter ablation.

Infective Endocarditis >9 times
Features Staphylococcus epidermidis on prosthetic valves and Duke's clinical criteria.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Infection of the endocardial surface of the heart, most commonly affecting heart valves (S. aureus, S. viridans, S. epidermidis).

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Fever, new heart murmur, Roth spots, Osler nodes, Janeway lesions, splinter hemorrhages.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Blood cultures (3 sets from different sites).
Diagnostic: Modified Duke Criteria; Transesophageal Echocardiogram (TEE) to visualize vegetations.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Empiric IV Antibiotics (Vancomycin + Ceftriaxone).
Definitive: Pathogen-tailored IV antibiotics for 4-6 weeks or surgical valve repair/replacement.

Cardiac Tamponade >8 times
Key diagnostic criteria center around Beck's Triad (hypotension, muffled/distant heart sounds, and elevated JVP).
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Life-threatening compression of cardiac chambers secondary to pericardial fluid accumulation leading to decreased venous return.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Beck's Triad (Hypotension, Distant heart sounds, Elevated JVP) and Pulsus Paradoxus (>10 mmHg drop in SBP on inspiration).

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: ECG (Electrical alternans, low voltage QRS).
Diagnostic: Echocardiogram showing right ventricular diastolic collapse.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: IV fluid bolus to increase preload.
Definitive: Immediate pericardiocentesis or pericardial window creation.

Left Axis Deviation >7 times
Highly tested on its common secondary causes, particularly chronic systemic hypertension.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

ECG finding where the mean electrical axis of the heart lies between -30° and -90°.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Asymptomatic directly; features depend on etiology (e.g., long-standing hypertension, aortic stenosis, LABB).

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: 12-lead ECG (Lead I positive, Lead aVF negative, Lead II negative).
Diagnostic: Echocardiogram to evaluate Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH).

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Manage underlying secondary etiology.
Definitive: Antihypertensive therapy (ACEi/ARBs) for hypertensive heart disease.

Primary vs. Secondary Hypertension >14 times
Focuses on identifying secondary endocrine causes like renal artery stenosis or pheochromocytoma.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Essential hypertension (90% cases, idiopathic) vs. Secondary hypertension (identifiable underlying cause like renal artery stenosis or Conn syndrome).

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Resistant hypertension (>3 drugs), young age of onset (<30 yrs), abdominal bruits, hypokalemia, or paroxysmal symptoms.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Basic metabolic panel, lipid panel, urinalysis.
Diagnostic: Renal Doppler US (Renal artery stenosis), Plasma Metanephrines (Pheochromocytoma), Aldosterone/Renin ratio.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Primary: Lifestyle + ACEi/ARB/CCBs/Thiazides.
Definitive: Treat underlying cause (e.g., Renal artery stenting, tumor resection).

Asthma & Status Asthmaticus >12 times
Evaluated via peak flow monitoring, spirometry FEV1/FVC ratios, first-line steroid controller therapy, and immediate nebulizer/ventilation protocols.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Chronic inflammatory airway disease with reversible airway obstruction, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and bronchospasm.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Wheezing, cough, dyspnea, chest tightness; silent chest indicates impending respiratory failure in Status Asthmaticus.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF).
Diagnostic: Spirometry showing FEV1/FVC <0.70 with >12% post-bronchodilator reversibility.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS)-Formoterol controller; SABA (Albuterol) for acute exacerbations.
Definitive: Nebulized Albuterol/Ipratropium + IV Corticosteroids + Magnesium sulfate for Status Asthmaticus.

Type 1 vs. Type 2 Respiratory Failure >10 times
Differentiated on ABGs by the presence of hypercapnia (retaining CO2) in Type 2.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Type 1 (Hypoxemic): PaO2 <60 mmHg with normal/low PaCO2. Type 2 (Hypercapnic): PaO2 <60 mmHg with PaCO2 >45 mmHg.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Type 1: Cyanosis, tachypnea, anxiety (ARDS, Pneumonia). Type 2: Somnolence, lethargy, asterixis, headache (COPD, Neuromuscular weakness).

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial & Diagnostic: Arterial Blood Gas (ABG) analysis to determine PaO2, PaCO2, and pH.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Type 1: High-flow supplemental O2.
Definitive: Type 2: Non-Invasive Positive Pressure Ventilation (BiPAP) or Endotracheal Intubation.

Primary Spontaneous Pneumothorax >8 times
Associated with tall, thin, young males due to the rupture of subpleural apical blebs.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Air accumulation in the pleural space without preceding trauma, typically due to rupture of subpleural apical emphysematous blebs.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Sudden onset pleuritic chest pain and dyspnea in a tall, thin male; decreased breath sounds and hyperresonance on affected side.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial & Diagnostic: Upright Chest X-ray showing visceral pleural line with absent lung markings peripherally.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Small (<2cm): Supplemental O2 and observation.
Definitive: Large (>2cm): Needle aspiration or Chest tube thoracostomy; VATS pleurodesis for recurrent cases.

Endocrinology & Metabolism

Graves' Disease >12 times
Tested on the Merseburg Triad (exophthalmos, goiter, tachycardia/tremor), TSI antibody assays, and preferred pregnancy drugs.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Autoimmune hyperthyroidism caused by Thyroid-Stimulating Immunoglobulins (TSI) activating TSH receptors.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Merseburg Triad: Exophthalmos, diffuse goiter, tachycardia/tremor. Pretibial myxedema, heat intolerance, weight loss.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: TSH (suppressed), Free T3/T4 (elevated).
Diagnostic: Positive TSI antibodies and diffuse high radioactive iodine uptake (RAIU).

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Methimazole (PTU in 1st trimester pregnancy) + Propranolol for symptoms.
Definitive: Radioactive Iodine Ablation (RAI) or Total Thyroidectomy.

Thyroid Storm >6 times
Emergency hyperthyroid scenario presenting with high fever, agitation, tachycardia, and atrial fibrillation.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Life-threatening hypermetabolic state triggered by infection, surgery, or trauma in patients with unmanaged hyperthyroidism.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

High fever (>104°F/40°C), severe tachycardia/AFib, delirium/agitation, jaundice, high-output heart failure.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Clinical diagnosis using Burch-Wartofsky Point Scale.
Diagnostic: Severely suppressed TSH and markedly elevated Free T3/T4.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Propranolol + PTU (high dose) followed 1 hour later by Potassium Iodide (SSKI) + Hydrocortisone.
Definitive: ICU admission and stabilization prior to thyroidectomy/RAI.

Hashimoto's Thyroiditis >9 times
Differentiated by metabolic slowing (weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation) and anti-TPO/anti-thyroglobulin antibodies.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Most common cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient regions; autoimmune destruction of thyroid parenchyma with Hurthle cells.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, non-tender diffuse goiter, delayed reflex relaxation.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Elevated TSH, decreased Free T4.
Diagnostic: High Anti-TPO (thyroid peroxidase) and Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line & Definitive: Lifelong Levothyroxine (T4) replacement therapy monitored via TSH level.

Subacute De Quervain's Thyroiditis >7 times
Features painful thyroid enlargement post-viral infection with high ESR and low radioactive iodine uptake.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Granulomatous thyroiditis occurring after a viral upper respiratory infection leading to transient hyperthyroidism followed by hypothyroidism.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Exquisitely painful, tender thyroid gland radiating to jaw/ears, fever, fatigue, transient hyperthyroid symptoms.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: High ESR/CRP, low TSH.
Diagnostic: Low Radioactive Iodine Uptake (RAIU) scan (<5%).

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line & Definitive: High-dose NSAIDs or Aspirin; Oral Prednisone for severe pain. Self-limiting disease.

Riedel Thyroiditis >6 times
A woody, stone-hard fixed neck mass mimicking malignancy, associated with systemic IgG4 disease.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Rare chronic inflammatory disease characterized by extensive fibrotic replacement of thyroid tissue extending into local structures (IgG4-related).

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

"Woody", stone-hard fixed non-tender thyroid mass, dyspnea, dysphagia, hoarseness due to local invasion.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Thyroid Ultrasound / CT neck.
Diagnostic: Open Surgical Biopsy (FNAC insufficient) showing dense fibrous tissue with IgG4 plasma cells.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Systemic Glucocorticoids (Prednisone) + Tamoxifen.
Definitive: Surgical decompression if airway is compromised.

Papillary Thyroid Carcinoma (PTC) >10 times
Key cytological features are Orphan Annie nuclei, nuclear grooves, and psammoma bodies, diagnosed via FNAC.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Most common thyroid malignancy (80%); linked to childhood radiation exposure, excellent prognosis.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Painless, solitary, hard thyroid nodule; cervical lymphadenopathy.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Thyroid US showing microcalcifications/irregular margins.
Diagnostic: Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology (FNAC) showing Orphan Annie eye nuclei, nuclear grooves, and Psammoma bodies.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line & Definitive: Surgical resection (Lobectomy or Total Thyroidectomy) followed by Radioactive Iodine (RAI) ablation and TSH suppression.

Follicular Thyroid Carcinoma vs. Adenoma >8 times
Distinguishing them requires demonstrating capsular or vascular invasion on histopathology (FNAC is insufficient).
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Second most common thyroid cancer; metastasizes hematogenously (bones, lungs). FNAC cannot distinguish benign adenoma from malignant carcinoma.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Solitary thyroid nodule, hematogenous bone/lung metastasis symptoms.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Thyroid US and FNAC showing follicular neoplasm.
Diagnostic: Post-operative Histopathology proving Capsular or Vascular Invasion.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line & Definitive: Total Thyroidectomy followed by Radioactive Iodine (131-I) ablation.

Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma (MTC) >9 times
Arises from parafollicular C-cells, secretes calcitonin, displays amyloid stroma, and is linked with RET mutations.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Neuroendocrine tumor originating from parafollicular C-cells; secretes Calcitonin; associated with MEN 2A and 2B (RET proto-oncogene).

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Thyroid nodule, diarrhea, flushing (due to Calcitonin/VIP secretion), neck lymphadenopathy.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Serum Calcitonin and CEA levels.
Diagnostic: FNAC showing polygonal cells with Congo Red positive Apple-Green birefringent amyloid deposits.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line & Definitive: Total Thyroidectomy with central neck lymph node dissection. Screen for Pheochromocytoma before surgery!

Anaplastic Thyroid Carcinoma >6 times
Presents as a highly aggressive, rapidly expanding neck mass in the elderly with pleomorphic giant and spindle cells.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Undifferentiated, highly malignant thyroid tumor occurring in elderly patients; almost 100% mortality rate.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Rapidly growing, hard, fixed neck mass causing severe dyspnea, dysphagia, hoarseness, and stridor.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: CT Neck/Chest.
Diagnostic: FNAC or Core Needle Biopsy showing pleomorphic giant cells, spindle cells, and necrosis.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line & Definitive: Palliative tracheostomy for airway security + Chemoradiation (surgical cure is rarely possible).

Cushing Syndrome & Disease >12 times
Screened via late-night salivary cortisol, 24-hr urinary free cortisol, or low-dose dexamethasone suppression testing.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Hypercortisolism due to exogenous steroids, adrenal adenoma (Syndrome), or ACTH-secreting pituitary adenoma (Disease).

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Moon facies, buffalo hump, abdominal purple striae, central obesity, hypertension, hyperglycemia, proximal muscle weakness.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial Screening: 24-hr urinary free cortisol OR Overnight 1mg Low-Dose Dexamethasone suppression test.
Diagnostic/Localization: ACTH level + High-Dose Dexamethasone test (suppresses pituitary Cushing Disease).

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Taper steroids (if exogenous).
Definitive: Transsphenoidal pituitary resection (Cushing Disease) or Adrenalectomy.

Primary Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's Disease) >9 times
Features hyperpigmentation, severe hypotension, and salt wasting (hyponatremia, hyperkalemia).
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Autoimmune or infectious (TB) destruction of all 3 layers of the adrenal cortex leading to aldosterone and cortisol deficiency.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Hyperpigmentation (palmar creases/buccal mucosa due to high MSH/ACTH), hypotension, postural dizziness, weight loss, salt craving.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Serum electrolytes (Hyponatremia, Hyperkalemia, Non-gap acidosis).
Diagnostic: Cosyntropin (ACTH) Stimulation Test (failure to raise cortisol >18 mcg/dL).

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line & Definitive: Glucocorticoid (Oral Hydrocortisone) + Mineralocorticoid (Fludrocortisone) replacement.

Adrenal Crisis >8 times
Emergency presentation of severe shock, treated immediately with aggressive IV fluids and high-dose Hydrocortisone.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Acute severe adrenal insufficiency triggered by stress, trauma, infection, or abrupt steroid withdrawal.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Refractory severe shock/hypotension, severe abdominal pain, vomiting, fever, confusion.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Random cortisol and ACTH (do NOT delay life-saving treatment for test results!).

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line & Definitive: Immediate IV High-dose Hydrocortisone (100mg bolus) + Aggressive IV Normal Saline (D5NS) resuscitation.

Conn's Syndrome (Primary Hyperaldosteronism) >8 times
Differentiated by refractory hypertension with profound hypokalemia and low plasma renin.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Autonomous excess production of aldosterone by an adrenal adenoma (Conn) or bilateral adrenal hyperplasia.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Refractory hypertension, muscle weakness, cramps, paresthesias, polyuria/polydipsia (due to hypokalemia).

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Plasma Aldosterone to Renin Ratio (PAC/PRA > 20 with Aldosterone >15).
Diagnostic: Oral/IV Saline Suppression test followed by Adrenal CT scan.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Spironolactone or Eplerenone (Aldosterone receptor antagonists).
Definitive: Surgical Laparoscopic Adrenalectomy for unilateral adenoma.

Pheochromocytoma >12 times
Presents with paroxysmal hypertension and the triad of headache, sweating, and palpitations; requires alpha before beta-blockade.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Catecholamine-secreting neuroendocrine tumor of chromaffin cells in the adrenal medulla (Rule of 10s).

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Classic Triad: Episodic Headache, Sweating (Diaphoresis), Tachycardia/Palpitations + Severe Paroxysmal Hypertension.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Plasma Free Fractionated Metanephrines or 24-hr urinary metanephrines.
Diagnostic: Abdominal/Adrenal CT or MRI scan.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Alpha-blocker (Phenoxybenzamine) FIRST, followed by Beta-blocker.
Definitive: Surgical Laparoscopic Adrenalectomy.

Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH) >7 times
Most commonly caused by a 21-hydroxylase deficiency, leading to ambiguous female genitalia and salt wasting.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Autosomal recessive deficiency of 21-hydroxylase enzyme blocking aldosterone/cortisol synthesis and shunting precursors to androgen production.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Ambiguous genitalia in 46,XX females; salt-wasting crisis (vomiting, hypotension, shock) in newborns at 1-2 weeks.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Newborn screen / Serum Electrolytes (Hyponatremia, Hyperkalemia).
Diagnostic: Markedly elevated 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP) levels.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line & Definitive: Exogenous Hydrocortisone (Glucocorticoid) and Fludrocortisone (Mineralocorticoid) to suppress excess ACTH.

Acromegaly >8 times
Screened using serum IGF-1 and confirmed by a failure of GH suppression during an Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT).
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Excess Growth Hormone (GH) secretion post-epiphyseal plate closure, usually due to a pituitary somatotroph macroadenoma.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Coarsening of facial features, enlarged hands/feet (ring/shoe size increase), jaw prognathism, macroglossia, hypertension, carpal tunnel.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial Screening: Serum Insulin-like Growth Factor 1 (IGF-1) level.
Diagnostic: Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT) showing failure of GH suppression <1 ng/mL; Pituitary MRI.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Somatostatin analogs (Octreotide/Lanreotide) or Pegvisomant.
Definitive: Transsphenoidal Surgical Resection of pituitary adenoma.

Prolactinoma >10 times
Presents with galactorrhea, amenorrhea, and visual field deficits; managed first-line with dopamine agonists like Cabergoline.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Most common functional pituitary adenoma secreting excess prolactin, suppressing GnRH.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Females: Oligomenorrhea/Amenorrhea, galactorrhea, infertility. Males: Decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, bitemporal hemianopsia.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Fasting Serum Prolactin level (>200 ng/mL strongly suggests prolactinoma).
Diagnostic: MRI Sella with contrast.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line & Definitive: Dopamine Agonists (Cabergoline or Bromocriptine). Surgery reserved for refractory/rapidly deteriorating cases.

Central vs. Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus >8 times
Differentiated by water deprivation followed by a desmopressin (DDAVP) response test.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Deficiency of ADH synthesis/release from hypothalamus/posterior pituitary (Central) vs. renal resistance to ADH (Nephrogenic, e.g., Lithium).

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Profound polyuria (dilute urine), intense polydipsia (craving ice-cold water), hypernatremia, dehydration.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Water Deprivation Test (urine osmolarity remains <300 mOsm/kg).
Diagnostic: Desmopressin (DDAVP) Test: Urine osmolarity rises >50% in Central; no change in Nephrogenic.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Central: Desmopressin (DDAVP).
Definitive: Nephrogenic: Hydrochlorothiazide + Indomethacin + stop offending drug (Lithium).

SIADH >10 times
Presents as euvolemic hyponatremia with concentrated urine; rapid correction risks Central Pontine Myelinolysis.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Excess unsuppressible ADH release causing water retention, expansion of extracellular fluid, and dilutional hyponatremia (Small cell lung Ca, SSRIs).

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Euvolemic hyponatremia: Confusion, seizures, lethargy, coma, cerebral edema (no peripheral edema).

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial & Diagnostic: Low serum sodium/osmolarity (<275 mOsm/kg), High urine osmolarity (>100 mOsm/kg), High urine sodium (>40 mEq/L).

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Fluid Restriction (<800 mL/day).
Definitive: Hypertonic (3%) Saline for severe symptoms (correct <8-10 mEq/L per 24 hrs to prevent Osmotic Demyelination Syndrome); Vaptans.

Primary Hyperparathyroidism >10 times
Presents with recurrent calcium stones and "bones, stones, abdominal groans, psychiatric overtones".
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Unregulated excess secretion of PTH, most commonly by a single parathyroid adenoma (85%).

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

"Bones, Stones, Groans, Overtones": Osteitis fibrosa cystica, nephrolithiasis, peptic ulcers/constipation, depression/confusion.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Elevated Serum Calcium, Low Serum Phosphate.
Diagnostic: Elevated Intact PTH; Sestamibi Scan for adenoma localization.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: IV Fluids + Cinacalcet (medical management).
Definitive: Parathyroidectomy of offending gland.

Hypocalcemic Tetany >10 times
Elicits positive Chvostek and Trousseau signs; treated immediately with IV Calcium Gluconate.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Neuromuscular excitability due to acutely low ionized serum calcium, post-thyroidectomy or in severe hypoparathyroidism/pancreatitis.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Chvostek sign (facial twitching tapping facial nerve), Trousseau sign (carpopedal spasm on BP cuff inflation), paresthesias, laryngospasm, prolonged QT interval.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Serum ionized calcium (<4.4 mg/dL) or total calcium (<8.5 mg/dL).
Diagnostic: ECG showing QT prolongations.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line & Definitive: Immediate IV Calcium Gluconate (10%); Oral Calcium + Vitamin D (Calcitriol) maintenance.

Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (Gastrinoma) >7 times
Multiple, intractable peptic ulcers with severe watery diarrhea.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Gastrin-secreting neuroendocrine tumor (duodenum/pancreas) leading to gastric acid hypersecretion; linked with MEN 1.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Recurrent, severe, refractory peptic ulcers (often distal duodenum/jejunum), GERD, and secretor watery diarrhea.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Fasting Serum Gastrin level (>1000 pg/mL).
Diagnostic: Secretin Stimulation Test (gastrin surges >200 pg/mL); Somatostatin Receptor Scintigraphy (OctreoScan).

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: High-dose Proton Pump Inhibitors (Omeprazole).
Definitive: Surgical Resection of gastrinoma.

Insulinoma >7 times
Characterised by the Whipple Triad (hypoglycemia symptoms, low blood glucose, and instant relief upon glucose administration).
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Benign neuroendocrine tumor of pancreatic beta-islet cells hypersecreting insulin autonomously.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Whipple Triad: 1. Hypoglycemic symptoms (sweating, tremor, neuroglycopenia), 2. Verified blood glucose <55 mg/dL, 3. Immediate resolution after glucose administration.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial & Diagnostic: 72-hour Fasting Test demonstrating elevated Insulin, C-peptide, and Proinsulin with low glucose.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Diazoxide or Octreotide to inhibit insulin release.
Definitive: Surgical Enucleation of pancreatic tumor.

Glucagonoma >6 times
Classically presents with a necrolytic migratory erythema rash, mild diabetes, and anemia.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Rare neuroendocrine tumor of pancreatic alpha cells hypersecreting glucagon.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

5 Ds: Dermatitis (Necrolytic Migratory Erythema), Diabetes mellitus, Diarrhea, DVT/Thromboembolism, Depression.

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: Fasting Serum Glucagon level (>500 pg/mL).
Diagnostic: Abdominal CT / MRI or Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS) for pancreatic mass.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Octreotide (Somatostatin analog) + Glycemic control.
Definitive: Surgical Resection.

Carcinoid Syndrome >8 times
Associated with primary appendix/ileum tumors metastasized to liver, elevated 24-hr urinary 5-HIAA.
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Systemic manifestation of neuroendocrine carcinoid tumors (appendix/ileum) secreting Serotonin after metastasizing to the liver.

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

Flushing, secretory watery diarrhea, bronchospasm/wheezing, right-sided heart valvular lesions (tricuspid regurgitation).

Initial & Diagnostic Tests

Initial: 24-hour Urinary 5-HIAA (5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid) excretion.
Diagnostic: Octreoscan (Somatostatin receptor scintigraphy) and CT abdomen.

1st Line & Definitive Treatment

1st Line: Octreotide / Lanreotide (Somatostatin receptor agonists).
Definitive: Surgical resection of primary tumor and hepatic metastases.

Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia (MEN) >10 times
MEN 1 (3 Ps) versus MEN 2A/2B (MTC, pheochromocytoma, and mucosal neuromas/marfanoid habitus).
Expand Detailed Clinical Information

Introduction / Definition

Autosomal dominant syndromes: MEN 1 (MEN1 gene), MEN 2A/2B (RET proto-oncogene mutations).

Clinical Signs & Symptoms

MEN 1: Parathyroid, Pancreas, Pituitary.
MEN 2A: Medullary Thyroid Ca, Pheochromocytoma, Parathyroid.
